Everything on this page comes from _data/resources.yml — sections in file order, items in file order — so adding material is a YAML entry and nothing else. Three fields are optional and the markup below is written for their absence: `signal` (the desk's own light the piece speaks to), `byline` (an author, which books have and videos do not) and `topics` (what to take from it). `take` — the desk's own paragraph on the piece — is optional to the template and mandatory in practice; resources.yml says why. ONE LINK PER ITEM, the headline. A second "watch it here" link under the same headline would be two names for one destination, which reads as two places to anyone tabbing or listening through the page — the same rule the download line on /dashboard/ follows. PAID LINKS: `affiliate: true` on a section marks every item in it; `featured: true` additionally sets it as a plate. The two are separate flags on purpose — a section can be paid without being promoted, and the disclosure must not depend on the decoration. Two things follow and neither is decoration. The chip prints AT the link, because § 5a Abs. 4 UWG wants the commercial purpose recognisable there and not only in the note above; and rel gains `sponsored nofollow`, which is Google's requirement for a paid link. The chip is deliberately NOT gold: gold is the slot the desk's own signal sits in, and a disclosure that borrows the accent colour reads as a recommendation. {%- endcomment -%}

The Reading Room

Where to learn the mechanics this desk reads — from people who are not this desk.

None of this is ChartHorizon's work. It is listed because it explains, better than a paragraph here could, what sits behind the signals the three-signal read runs on: what open interest is doing when price is doing nothing, how a commercial hedging program actually behaves, and what a spread chart tells you that a price chart does not.

{%- comment -%} This second paragraph appeared with the first paid links (2026-09-06). The original said flatly that nothing here was an endorsement, which a commission makes untrue — so it says what is actually the case instead, above the fold and before the first link. {%- endcomment -%}

Some of the links are paid, and each one says so where it sits. The desk earns a commission if you buy through one; the price you pay is the same either way. The shelf leads this page because it pays — that is the arrangement, said here rather than left to be worked out. It changes nothing about what is in the books, and nothing about the standing rule: take the mechanics and check them against your own screen.
