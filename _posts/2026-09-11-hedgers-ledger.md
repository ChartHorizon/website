The report dated 8 September puts nine markets at the edge of their three-year positioning range: six at lows, three at highs. The low side is last week's farm cluster, intact — Corn, Wheat, Soybeans and Cotton at the 1st percentile, Sugar at a fresh three-year record — with Copper added to it; Corn, Wheat and Cotton came off the records they set a week ago, but none of last week's five lows has left the board. All six trade in the upper third of their one-year price range, which is where producers sell forward, so a hedger short this deep describes the rally more than it predicts its end. The three highs are all new: the E-mini Russell 2000, the 30-Year T-Bond and Class III Milk. Lean Hogs, last week's only high, dropped back out of the top five percent.

## The hedging program

Where each market's tracked COT net sits inside its own trailing six-month range — above the midpoint of that range or below it. This is the same positioning read at close quarters, and it answers a different question from the extremes above: a book can sit at a three-year low and still have been working higher for six months. What follows is only what MOVED — the markets that crossed that midpoint with this week's report.

16 programs read above the midpoint of their own trailing six-month range this week, 23 below. Three crossed it with this report — Platinum from above to below, Soybean Oil from below to above and Japanese Yen from above to below. Two of the three undo turns made in [last week's issue](/2026/09/05/hedgers-ledger/): Soybean Oil had crossed downward then, and the yen upward.

### Japanese Yen — the program turns bearish

![Japanese Yen — the six-month hedging program, commercials net]({{ page.cards }}/prog_jpy_fx.webp){: width="1200" height="1036" loading="lazy"}

In the CFTC report of 2026-09-08, Commercial Net crossed the midpoint of its own trailing six-month range: it now sits below that midpoint, where the previous report still had it above. It cleared that midpoint by 50.0% of the window's own range — which is all the distance there is: the net now sits at the bottom of its six-month window.

It got there in one report: Commercial Net fell from +97,561 contracts to −14,041, the largest one-week move for this market in an archive that starts in June 2021, while the yen futures price rose 4.2%. This is the book whose rebuild [The Cheaper Instrument](/2026/09/06/the-cheaper-instrument/) counted on 6 September as a test met, naming a long that keeps climbing through the Bank of Japan's meeting on the 18th as the confirmation to watch; a week before that meeting, the book is net short. The report was taken in the week before the September contract expires, when a roll can swell open interest (up 87,753 here), but across the 20 earlier reports from that week the net never moved more than 30,963 contracts — and the report dated 15 September, the first without that contract, is published on the day of the meeting.
